## 1. HOOK & INTRODUCTION (2 min)

**Opening line:**
"Think about the last lesson you taught that students still talk about weeks later. What made it stick? I'd bet it wasn't the lecture — it was the moment they *did* something, *discussed* something, or *discovered* something."

**Framing statement:**
- Today isn't about abandoning what works — it's about combining explanation, questioning, discussion, collaboration, and technology so every student engages, not just the ones already listening.
- Goal by the end: you leave with a practical toolkit you can use in your very next lesson — not just theory.

**Quick interactive opener (30 sec):**
Ask for a show of hands: *"Who has taught a topic three different ways because the first two didn't land?"* — Use this to bridge into learner-centred teaching.

## 2. LEARNER-CENTRED VS TEACHER-CENTRED TEACHING (3 min)

**Core idea:** The difference isn't just *who talks more* — it's *who does the thinking.*

| Teacher-Centred | Learner-Centred |
|---|---|
| Teacher explains, students listen | Teacher guides, students construct understanding |
| One-way information flow | Two-way dialogue and interaction |
| Passive note-taking | Active problem-solving, discussion, application |
| Assessment = recall | Assessment = understanding & application |

**Why it matters:**
- Retention research consistently shows students remember far more of what they *do* and *discuss* than what they only *hear*.
- Learner-centred does NOT mean "no teacher input" — it means the teacher designs the experience, then steps back to let students engage with it.

**Quick example (use a subject familiar to the room):**
- Teacher-centred: "Today I will explain photosynthesis." (teacher talks 20 min)
- Learner-centred: "Here's a plant kept in the dark for a week and one in sunlight — what do you notice? Why do you think that happened?" (students investigate, then teacher fills gaps)

**Facilitator note:** Ask 2-3 teachers to briefly share one topic they currently teach the "traditional" way, and float one learner-centred alternative live.

## 3. ACTIVE LEARNING & COLLABORATIVE STRATEGIES (4 min)

**Definition:** Strategies where students process, discuss, or apply content rather than passively receive it.

**Practical strategies teachers can use tomorrow:**

1. **Think-Pair-Share**
   - Pose a question → students think alone (1 min) → discuss with a partner (2 min) → share with class.
   - Works for ages 6 to 16; scales in complexity.

2. **Group Work with Assigned Roles**
   - Give each group member a specific job (recorder, presenter, timekeeper, questioner) so group work doesn't collapse into one student doing everything.

3. **Peer Teaching ("Each One Teach One")**
   - Split a topic into parts; each student/group masters one part and teaches it to others.
   - Deepens understanding — teaching something is the strongest form of learning it.

4. **Jigsaw Method**
   - Students become "experts" in a sub-topic in home groups, then regroup to teach each other the full picture.

**Practical tip for large classes (common in many Nigerian schools):**
- Use "turn-and-talk" with the student next to them — no need to physically move desks. Takes 60 seconds and re-engages the whole room.

## 4. EFFECTIVE QUESTIONING TECHNIQUES (4 min)

**Core principle:** The quality of your questions determines the depth of student thinking.

**Move beyond "yes/no" and recall questions using this simple ladder:**

| Level | Question Starter | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Recall | "What is...?" | What is the capital of Nigeria? |
| Understanding | "Why do you think...?" | Why do you think Lagos was once the capital? |
| Application | "How would you use...?" | How would you plan a city to avoid Lagos's traffic problems? |
| Analysis/Evaluation | "What would happen if...?" / "Which is better, and why?" | What would happen if we moved the capital again today? |

**Practical techniques:**
- **Wait time:** After asking a question, pause 3–5 seconds before calling on anyone. This alone increases the quality of answers dramatically.
- **No hands up (cold-calling fairly):** Use popsicle sticks/names in a bowl so all students stay mentally engaged, not just volunteers.
- **"Why/How" follow-ups:** Whatever a student answers, ask "why do you say that?" — pushes reasoning, not just correctness.
- **Redirect, don't rescue:** If an answer is wrong, ask another student "What do you think about that answer?" instead of correcting immediately.

**Facilitator note:** Demonstrate wait time live — ask the room a question and visibly count 5 seconds in silence. Teachers feel how uncomfortable (and powerful) it is.

## 5. ACTIVITY-BASED & HANDS-ON TEACHING APPROACHES (4 min)

**Why it works:** Concepts become concrete and memorable when students manipulate, build, or experience them — not just hear about them.

**Practical, low-resource examples (primary & secondary):**

- **Primary:** Teaching fractions with orange slices or folded paper instead of only diagrams on the board.
- **Primary:** Acting out a story's plot (role play) instead of only reading it aloud.
- **Secondary:** Simulating a market/economy in class (buying, selling, supply & demand) to teach basic economics.
- **Secondary (Science):** A simple, low-cost experiment (e.g., vinegar + baking soda for a chemical reaction) instead of only describing reactions.
- **Any subject:** Case scenarios — give students a real-world problem/situation and ask them to solve it using the day's lesson.

**Key facilitator message:** "Hands-on" doesn't require expensive lab equipment — everyday objects, role play, and real scenarios work in any classroom, with or without resources.

## 6. DIFFERENTIATING INSTRUCTION (4 min)

**Core idea:** Students in the same class differ in ability, pace, and learning style — one-size-fits-all teaching leaves some behind and bores others.

**Three practical entry points for differentiation:**

1. **Differentiate by Content/Task Difficulty**
   - Give tiered tasks on the same topic: a basic version, a standard version, and a stretch/challenge version. Everyone works on the same topic, at their level.

2. **Differentiate by Support**
   - Provide worked examples, sentence starters, or visual aids for students who need more scaffolding; give open-ended challenges for faster finishers.

3. **Differentiate by Grouping**
   - Mix ability groups for peer support on some tasks; use similar-ability groups for targeted practice on others.

**Practical classroom tip:**
- Prepare 2–3 "extension questions" in advance for fast finishers, so they don't sit idle while others catch up — idle time is where classroom management problems start.

**Facilitator note:** Emphasize this is manageable even with large class sizes — it doesn't require creating 30 different lesson plans, just flexible tasks and questions.

## 7. USING TECHNOLOGY WISELY — A SUPPORT TOOL, NOT A REPLACEMENT (3 min)

**Core message:** Technology should amplify good teaching, not substitute for it.

**Practical, low-cost applications:**
- **Phone/projector:** Short (2–3 min) videos or images to introduce or clarify a concept before discussion — not to replace the discussion itself.
- **Quiz apps (Kahoot, Google Forms, or even a simple WhatsApp poll):** Quick formative checks for understanding at the end of a lesson.
- **Audio/visual aids:** For schools without internet, even a printed diagram, radio recording, or pre-downloaded video on a phone counts as "technology used wisely."

**Caution to raise:**
- Technology should never be used just because it's available — always ask: *"Does this tool help students understand better, or is it a distraction?"*

---

## 8. WRAP-UP & KEY TAKEAWAYS (1 min)

**The five things to remember:**
1. Shift from "telling" to "guiding" — let students do the thinking.
2. Use active strategies (Think-Pair-Share, group roles, peer teaching) to engage everyone, not just volunteers.
3. Ask better questions, and give students time to think before answering.
4. Make lessons hands-on wherever possible — even with simple, everyday materials.
5. Differentiate tasks and support so no student is bored or left behind; use technology only where it adds real value.

**Closing line:**
"You don't need to change everything about how you teach tomorrow. Pick ONE strategy from today — a better question, a Think-Pair-Share, one hands-on activity — and try it in your very next lesson. Small, consistent shifts are what transform a classroom."
